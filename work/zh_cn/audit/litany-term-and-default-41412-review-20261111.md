# Litany Terminology and `default:41412` Review

- Date: 2026-11-11.
- Scope: `base` and `default` terminology for `Litany`/`Litanies`, plus
  Korean naturalness and register at `default:41412`.
- English is the meaning reference; Japanese is the primary reference for
  style and register; Chinese is supporting evidence.

## Litany Terminology

The established Korean form for the collectible and magical `Litany` text is
`연도문`. All `base` rows whose English source names a Litany already used
this form: all eight source rows. A full English-source comparison found 36
`Litany`/`Litanies` mentions in `default`; 34 refer to the collectible or
magical texts and use `연도문`. The other two mention only the `Litany Altar`
and do not refer to a Litany text. In `default`, the following 18 IDs were
aligned to `연도문`:

`6433`, `16687`, `20294`, `20802`, `28370`, `44961`, `45085`, `50073`,
`50459`, `51341`, `53108`, `54628`, `55032`, `62087`, `62181`, `62372`,
`63112`, `63578`.

The edit preserves the distinction between `Litany` and ordinary `prayer`.
Generic prayers, prayer scrolls, prayer runes, and the `Prayer of St. Eadric`
remain `기도문` where the English source says `prayer` rather than
`litany`. Japanese consistently identifies the collectible objects as
`連祷書`; several descriptions also establish that they are volumes, so
references to multiple collectible Litanies use `권`.
No `연도서` remains in either active `base` or `default`, and no English
`prayer` row in these resources was incorrectly rendered as `연도문`.

## `default:41412`

English: “The Jottun have wronged the Kollossae in the past, but the
Kollossae have a shared history with them. It was right to grant them mercy.”

Japanese: “ジョタンはかつてカロッサエを苦しめたが、カロッサエは彼らと
同じ歴史を共有している。彼らに情けをかけるのは正しいことだった”

The prior Korean wording shifted into a stiff `공유하고 있다` construction
and left the referent of `그들` implicit. It now reads:

`요툰(Jottun)은 과거 콜로사이(Kollossae)에게 잘못을 저질렀지만, 콜로사이는 요툰과 오랜 역사를 함께해 왔어. 요툰에게 자비를 베푼 건 옳은 선택이었어.`

This keeps the plain register supported by Japanese, clarifies that the
Jottun are the recipients of mercy, and preserves the meaning of the English.
No individual speaker or playback order is inferred from the asset association.
