## Decisions

- ID 10908 is `not an issue`: the Korean preserves the Fae's changing attitude
  toward death, the unreliable regeneration, the Great Cycle, and the
  resulting fear of extinction. It follows the apparent English meaning
  without reproducing the source's grammatical slips as translation errors.
- ID 10911 is `not an issue`: `달 야영지` preserves the capitalized Moon Camp
  as a place and retains the invitation to dance, laugh, and sing. No
  conflicting established Korean place-name is available.
- ID 10916 remains uncertain: `honor my queen` can denote honoring the queen
  or an allegiance-based duty. Japanese supports honoring her, while the
  current `충성을 다하다` emphasizes loyalty; context does not settle the
  distinction, so the text remains unchanged.
- ID 10917 is `not an issue`: the shipwreck, loss of most of the crew, three
  survivors, and route through the flooded cavern to the mainland are
  preserved.
- ID 10924 is `not an issue`: the harsher punishment of the speaker's own
  group and the three outcomes—lashing, branding, and expulsion—are retained.
- ID 10925 is `not an issue`: `페이 몰이꾼` is a plausible rendering of Fae
  beaters in a hunt, and the restriction of the enchantments to Fae royalty
  and archsages is preserved. The exact hunting role remains a terminology
  question, not a demonstrated row-level mistranslation.
- ID 10928 is `not an issue`: `조각가` preserves the literal Carver image in
  the poem. Japanese's creator-like paraphrase and capitalization leave the
  title status open, but do not establish that the Korean meaning is wrong.
  The poem's three-line layout and first whisper, “Mortals,” are retained.
- ID 10938 is `not an issue`: `온순하다` conveys the meek/docile reading
  supported by English and Chinese, while the following troll comparison
  preserves the teasing contrast and the polite Korean voice.
- ID 10942 is accepted: replace the unsupported “beautiful and majestic
  blessing” image with the source's idea of adding beauty and magnificence;
  replace “a warning to all” with “a reminder to all,” preserving the
  metaphor of mortal mundanity.
